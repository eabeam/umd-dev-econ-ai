# AI Use Policy

From the course assignment, plus the reasoning behind it.

## The rules

**You may use AI for:**
- Programming assistance, debugging, and explaining documentation
- Brainstorming analytical approaches
- Proofreading your write-ups

**You may not use AI to:**
- Generate the text that you submit
- Do your interpretation for you — reading tables, drawing conclusions, and the analytical thinking are yours

**Always:** you remain responsible for verifying every result and understanding every line of your code.

**Git/GitHub** is encouraged but not required — you will not be penalized for skipping it.

## Why the line is drawn there

The coding is a means; the economics is the end. AI is genuinely good at the means — writing a loop, explaining a merge error, drafting a cleaning file. If it also does the interpretation, the course has nothing left to teach you, and your future self has nothing to build on.

Two failure modes to watch for in AI-written code (we cover both in the guest lecture):

1. **Code that isn't wrong but isn't readable.** You (and your future coauthors) must be able to open a file and follow it.
2. **Silent research decisions.** AI code will happily impute missing values, drop observations, or recode outliers without telling you — choices that are *research decisions*, not programming details. Log them: see the [research decisions log template](materials/cleaning_decision_log.md).

The enforcement mechanism is mostly you: the in-class discussions work only if the tables are yours to explain.
